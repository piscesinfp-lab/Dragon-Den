Monitor Setup

Three-Screen Command

Two ViewSonic VA2247 (1080p/100hz) flanking one AOC (1440p/60hz) center. The resolution difference is actually fine — your main gaming and work happens center, the flankers are reference screens.

The Mixed Resolution Strategy

Put the AOC 1440p dead center — this is your primary gaming, streaming, and work monitor. Left VA2247: communication apps, Discord, OBS dashboard, reference material. Right VA2247: browser, Spotify, secondary content, or a vertical flip for code/docs. You'll never need to move your primary gaze off the 1440p center.

🖥️

Triple Monitor Arm — The Key Purchase

A single arm that holds all three and articulates independently. This frees your entire desk surface and lets you adjust each screen independently for gaming vs work mode.

  • Recommended: VIVO STAND-V003T — triple monitor arm, handles mixed sizes, full articulation per screen
  • Alternatively: Ergotron LX Triple — premium, buttery smooth, worth the extra cost
  • Clamp mounts to desk edge — no drilling required
  • Make sure your desk is at least 1.5" thick for the clamp to bite — if not, use a grommet mount
  • Weight limit: check your AOC specs — most 27" monitors are under 15lbs, within arm limits
📐

Monitor Positioning

  • Center AOC: Eye level, arm's reach — top of screen at or just below eye height
  • Left VA2247: Slight inward angle (15–20°), same height as center or slightly lower
  • Right VA2247: Mirror the left — slight inward angle
  • Total spread: ~55–60 inches — fits your desk with room for keyboard and peripherals in front
  • All three pushed back toward the wall — maximize desk depth in front of you
  • Bias lighting strip runs along the back of all three as one continuous strip
⚡

Display Settings to Dial In

  • AOC center: set as primary display, native 2560×1440, max refresh
  • VA2247 left + right: 1920×1080, 100hz — enable in Windows Display Settings
  • Match brightness across all three — start with the 1440p and calibrate the 1080p screens to match
  • Night light/warm mode on VA2247s if they read slightly cooler than the AOC
  • In Windows: set "Extend these displays" — drag screens in Settings to match physical layout

PC Placement

Full Tower Position

Floor left of the desk, elevated on a PC stand, RGB side panel facing into the room. Visible, protected, ventilated, and becomes part of the aesthetic.

🖥️

PC Stand / Riser

  • Elevates the tower 3–5" off the floor — protects from dust, vacuuming accidents, and carpet static
  • Look for a riser with wheels (casters) — easy to pull out for maintenance without scratching the floor
  • Recommended: OMOTON PC Tower Stand with casters — ~$25
  • Position it so the tempered glass RGB side panel faces into the room, not the wall
  • Leave 6"+ clearance behind for exhaust airflow — don't push it flat against the wall
🌡️

Airflow + Thermals

  • A-frame rooms can get warm in summer — the ceiling fan in the loft helps, but your room has its own consideration
  • Don't block the PC's intake (usually front/bottom) with cables or the wall
  • A small USB desk fan pointed at the PC side panel during heavy gaming sessions costs $10 and saves thermals
  • Cable raceway keeps the floor area under the desk clear — hot air needs somewhere to go
✨

PC as Aesthetic Object

  • The RGB inside the tower becomes Zone 5 of your lighting system — it glows against the Tricorn Black wall
  • If your tower RGB is iCUE (Corsair) or Razer Synapse — sync it to match your desk mood scene
  • A dark matte cable sleeve for the power cable from PC to surge protector finishes the look
  • If you ever upgrade: a case with a large tempered glass side panel maximizes the RGB display effect
Audio

Sound System

You have both a headset and speakers already. The goal is getting them working together cleanly — right output for the right context — without a rats nest of audio cables.

🔊

Speaker Placement

  • Speakers go on the floating shelf above the desk, one each side — angled slightly downward toward your seated ear level
  • This keeps them off the desk surface (frees space) and puts them at a better listening height
  • If speakers are bookshelf-style, small isolation pads under them prevent the shelf from resonating
  • The dark Tricorn Black wall behind actually helps with acoustics — absorbs some reflection
🎧

Headset Management

  • Headset stand on the right side of the desk — keeps it off the surface and within reach
  • A stand with a built-in USB hub is worth the $5 extra — charges the headset and adds ports
  • If wireless: receiver USB dongle goes directly into the back of the PC, not a hub (cleaner signal)
  • Headset cable (if wired): routed through the cable raceway with the rest, loops up to the right side of the desk
🎙️

Microphone — For Streaming

  • A boom arm mic mount is the move for a 3-monitor setup — keeps the mic out of the way when not streaming, swings into position when you are
  • If upgrading: Blue Yeti on a boom arm is the classic starter — ~$100 used. Elgato Wave:3 is cleaner for streaming
  • Mount the boom arm at the left edge of the desk — swing over center when streaming, park to the side when gaming
  • A small acoustic foam panel on the wall behind you helps with streaming audio in a hard-walled room
🔌

Audio Routing

  • A simple USB audio interface (Focusrite Scarlett Solo ~$120) lets you switch between speakers and headset cleanly with one knob
  • Alternatively: Windows Sound settings → set speakers as default, headset as communications device — system auto-routes game audio to speakers, Discord/calls to headset
  • For streaming: OBS can route different audio sources to different tracks — game audio, mic, Discord all separate
  • Voicemeeter Banana (free app) gives you a full virtual mixer if you want granular control without hardware

Cable Management

Zero Visible Cable Plan

Three monitors, a full tower, speakers, peripherals, and RGB strips generate a lot of cable. Here's exactly how every cable disappears.

Monitor cables × 3
DisplayPort/HDMI runs behind monitor arm post → down into desk grommet hole or over back edge → into cable raceway under desk → to PC ports. Velcro tie each at the arm.
PC power cable
Dark cable sleeve from PC to surge protector. Surge protector sits directly behind and slightly left of PC tower, against the wall. One cable up the wall raceway to the outlet.
Govee bias strip (USB)
USB-A into back of PC or powered USB hub. Strip cable routes behind monitors, tacked with adhesive cable clips every 12".
Speaker cables
Run from shelf down the wall inside the cable raceway. 3.5mm or RCA into audio interface or PC rear audio out. No cable visible on desk surface.
Headset (if wired)
Loop through cable raceway under desk, emerge at right side where headset stand sits. No loose cable on desk surface.
USB hub
Mounted under desk surface with adhesive velcro. USB-A into PC. All peripherals plug into the hub — one cable to PC. Hub lives under desk, invisible.
Keyboard + Mouse
Wireless strongly recommended — eliminates 2 cables entirely. If wired: route through desk grommet, velcro tie under desk surface to the USB hub.
Shelf RGB strips
Each Govee strip's power brick mounts under the shelf with velcro. USB cable tacked along shelf underside to the nearest outlet or smart power strip above the desk.
The Cable Management Kit

You need: one paintable cable raceway (paint it Tricorn Black) · adhesive cable clips · velcro ties (reusable, not zip ties) · a short USB hub mounted under the desk · one cable sleeve for the PC power cable. Total cost: ~$25–35. This is the single most ADHD-friendly upgrade on the list — a clean desk surface means a clear mind.

Seating

The Throne Upgrade Path

Your white chair is the placeholder. Here's the upgrade path that fits the aesthetic and your body — for long WFH + gaming sessions, this matters more than any peripheral.

Now — Use What You Have
White Chair + Dark Throw
Drape a dark charcoal or forest green throw over the back and seat. Adds a black cushion cover if it's fabric. It'll read darker in photos and in person until you upgrade. Costs $0.
Phase 2 — Mid Range (~$200–350)
Ergonomic Mesh Chair — Dark Colorway
Humanscale Freedom, Sihoo M57, or Branch Ergonomic Chair in all-black or dark grey. Mesh breathes during long sessions. Far better for your back than a gaming chair for WFH hours. Avoid: most RGB gaming chairs — they're gimmicky and uncomfortable for long sits.
Phase 3 — The Dream (~$500+)
Herman Miller Aeron or Secretlab Titan
If you stream professionally or WFH full time, this pays for itself in posture and focus. The Secretlab Titan fits the dragon aesthetic — they have limited edition fantasy colorways. Check Facebook Marketplace for used Aerons — they come up regularly for $200–300 and last 20 years.
Future Planning

Steam VR Readiness

You're planning for Steam VR and a Steam Machine for the loft. Here's how to design your bedroom setup now so VR fits without chaos later.

🥽

VR Space in Your Bedroom

  • The Murphy bed is your secret weapon — fold it up and you have ~10×10ft of clear floor space
  • That's enough for room-scale VR (Valve Index, Meta Quest, Vive all need ~6.5×6.5ft minimum)
  • The dark rug under your desk zone defines where VR is NOT — play in the center open zone
  • Base stations or sensors mount on the wall or beam with small screws — your nail access makes this easy
🎮

Steam Machine — Loft Plan

  • A Steam Machine (or a small form-factor PC running SteamOS) in the loft connects to a TV above the DnD table or on an adjacent wall
  • This separates couch gaming from desk gaming — loft becomes the chill/social gaming zone
  • A 43–55" TV mounted on the loft wall above or beside the DnD table does double duty: gaming + DnD map projection
  • Steam Link app lets you stream from your bedroom PC to the loft TV wirelessly — free, zero extra hardware needed right now
📡

Internet + Router Placement

  • Router should be as close to your PC as possible — ideally in your bedroom or just outside it
  • If the router is downstairs: a TP-Link Powerline adapter (~$40) runs ethernet through the house wiring — no drilling, stable connection
  • Wired ethernet to your PC always beats WiFi for gaming and streaming — run a flat ethernet cable along the baseboard if needed
  • For the loft Steam setup: a WiFi 6 router handles streaming from bedroom PC to loft TV wirelessly without lag

Working With What's There

The A-frame has warm honey-toned wood floors, exposed beams, and existing cream/olive walls. Don't fight them — use them. The floors ARE the bark tone. The beams ARE your natural wood element. Your job is to darken the atmosphere through lighting, textiles, and decor — not necessarily paint.

Bedroom · 13'6" × 10'4"

Wall-by-Wall Plan

Four walls, each with a job. Every inch of this room is assigned a purpose before a single piece of furniture arrives.

North Wall — The Anchor

13'6" wide · full height

The wall your eyes land on when you look up from your desk. This becomes your primary statement wall — deep color or dark removable wallpaper if painting isn't allowed.

  • Treatment: Dark removable wallpaper — forest green, dark botanical, or subtle dragon scale texture (available on Etsy, Tempaper brand)
  • Alternative if renting: Large dark tapestry or a gallery wall of fantasy/dragon art in dark frames — fills the wall without a single nail hole
  • Nothing else on this wall — it's the backdrop, not storage

South Wall — Murphy Bed Command

13'6" wide · flat interior wall = full height clearance

The south wall is your flat wall — no slope, full height. This is the ONLY correct wall for a Murphy bed in an A-frame. The sloped walls cannot accommodate a standard unit.

  • Murphy bed unit: Centered or offset left — 6ft wide unit fits with room to spare. Choose a unit with integrated side shelving that folds WITH the bed
  • Left of bed unit: Small vertical shelf or floating side table — stays clear of the bed's swing path
  • Right of bed unit: Access to bathroom — keep clear
  • Bedding when closed: Deep forest green velvet duvet, black fitted sheet, ember-toned throw pillow
  • Bed open: Headboard faces north — you wake up looking at your statement wall

West Wall — Desk + Window Zone

10'4" deep · window toward north end

Your window sits on this wall — natural light floods your desk during the day, your RGB glow takes over at night. The desk runs along this wall toward the window.

  • Desk placement: Your existing desk runs parallel to the west wall, perpendicular to the window — so you get side-lighting rather than glare in your face
  • Window treatment: Blackout curtains in dark forest green or charcoal — thermal lined, floor to ceiling even if the window is smaller. Creates the illusion of a larger window and keeps the room dark for sleep
  • Above desk: Your floating shelf with LED underlighting — holds small dragon pieces, a plant, speakers
  • Monitor position: Dual arm mount, monitors face south into the room — your back is to the window (no backlight glare)
  • Cable management: Cable spine down the wall, desk tray underneath. ADHD brain needs zero visible cables

East Wall — Dragon Shrine

10'4" deep · large closet at north end

The dragon wall. The first thing you see when you enter the room. Three staggered floating shelves, LED-underlit, housing your growing collection. The closet at the north end is boxed off — the remaining wall is yours.

  • Shelf layout: Three shelves at 28", 42", and 56" height — staggered in depth (deepest on bottom, shallowest on top)
  • Shelf length: ~4ft each — sized to the wall space between door and closet
  • LED strips: Warm amber OR RGB under each shelf — Govee RGBIC strips let you do both
  • The shrine: Top shelf center gets your best piece, slightly elevated on a small wooden stand. Spotlight it with a small directional LED puck
  • Closet treatment: Replace the louvered closet doors with a dark curtain rod + curtain — looks intentional, easier to access, hides clutter

A-Frame Architecture

Working the Sloped Ceiling

The sloped ceiling is your biggest design asset, not a limitation. No flat apartment has this. Lean into it completely.

🏔️

What the Slope Gives You

  • Drama. Walking in feels like entering a forest cabin or a wizard's tower
  • The exposed beams are already your dark wood accent — don't cover them
  • Peak height at center gives you space for a pendant light that hangs dramatically low
  • Angled walls = cozy, cave-like energy on the lower sides
💡

Lighting the Slope

  • Hang a single pendant from the peak — let it hang long (3–4ft drop). Rattan cage or dark metal
  • LED strips along the beam undersides — warm amber, runs the length of each beam
  • Avoid overhead flush lights — they kill the mood. Use the pendant + floor lamps only
  • Galaxy projector aimed at the slope = a forest canopy ceiling when in chill mode
📐

Furniture + the Slope

  • Murphy bed ONLY on the south flat wall — never on a sloped wall
  • Keep tall furniture (shelves, closet) away from the lower angled edges — it'll fight the ceiling
  • Low-profile furniture under the slopes — a small bench, floor cushion, or plant cluster works perfectly where the ceiling meets the wall
  • The center of the room stays clear — let the height breathe
🌿

Embracing the A-Frame

  • Hang a macramé or trailing plant from one of the beams near the peak
  • The angled walls can have small art — lean a framed piece against them rather than hanging
  • A single large dragon sculpture near the peak (on a high shelf) would be incredible — eye-level from the bed, lit from below
  • Dark paint/wallpaper on the triangular end walls deepens the forest-cave feel
Wood Tones

Working With the Existing Wood

The A-frame has warm honey/amber wood floors and beams. This is your biggest gift — real wood in a rental is rare. Here's exactly how to harmonize everything with it.

🪵

The Floor (Warm Honey Wood)

  • Don't fight it. Dark rugs in forest green, charcoal, or deep burgundy layer on top and create contrast without clashing
  • A 5×7 or 6×9 dark jute rug or vintage-style wool rug under the bed zone anchors the space
  • A smaller textured rug under the desk chair protects the floor and adds visual weight to the workzone
  • The wood floor peeking around rug edges keeps the room warm — don't cover it all
✨

The Exposed Beams

  • Leave them completely natural — they're the hero of the room
  • Add LED strips to the underside of each beam (Govee or generic RGBIC, 3M tape back)
  • In amber/candlelight mode they look like firelight. In green they feel like bioluminescent forest. In red — dragon lair
  • You can drape very thin fairy lights along a beam casually — looks intentional
🪑

Your Furniture Choices

  • Dark wood preferred — walnut, espresso, dark oak. Echoes the floor without matching exactly
  • Avoid: natural pine, light maple, or white furniture (clashes with the moody direction)
  • Your plain desk: wrap in dark wood contact paper (d-c-fix brand is excellent, ~$20) or dark matte spray paint
  • Matte black metal accents (lamp base, shelf brackets) bridge between the dark wood and the floor
🎨

Textiles + the Wood

  • Velvet and linen in forest green / deep charcoal / ember read luxe against warm wood
  • Avoid cool-toned fabrics (gray-blue, silver) — they fight the warm floor tone
  • Faux fur throw (white or cream) on the bed is a stunning contrast — the room Inspo had this and it works
  • Bamboo, rattan, and woven textures in decor tie the natural wood story together
The Harmony Formula

Warm honey wood floor + dark espresso/walnut furniture + forest green/charcoal textiles + ember RGB accent = a room that feels like a candlelit forest at night and a bright forest clearing by day. The warm and dark balance each other. Neither fights the other.

Lighting System

Layered Mood Lighting

Five zones, one app, infinite moods. The A-frame beams and sloped ceiling make this room a lighting showcase — use it.

💡

Zone 1 — Beam Underlighting

  • Govee RGBIC strips adhered to underside of each exposed beam
  • Default: warm amber 2700K — looks like candlelight on wood
  • Syncs to gaming or mood via app
  • ~$15–25 per 5m strip, 3M tape backing
🕯️

Zone 2 — Peak Pendant

  • Single pendant at the A-frame peak, hanging 3–4ft down
  • Rattan cage or dark metal style with Edison bulb
  • Smart bulb inside — dimmable, color temp adjustable
  • This is your "main" light — everything else is atmosphere
🐉

Zone 3 — Dragon Shelf RGB

  • Govee RGBIC under each floating shelf edge
  • Directional LED puck on shrine piece
  • Default: ember/amber warm tone
  • Dragon mode: deep red — makes figures look incredible
🖥️

Zone 4 — Desk RGB

  • Strip behind monitors (bias lighting — reduces eye strain)
  • Strip under desk edge facing room
  • Govee TV Backlight or Elgato Light Strip for monitor bias
  • Govee Sync Box if you want it reactive to on-screen content
🌌

Zone 5 — Galaxy Projector

  • Aimed at the sloped ceiling — instant forest canopy or galaxy sky
  • BlissLights Ark or Encalife Atmosphere recommended
  • Pairs with the beam lighting for a fully layered ceiling effect
  • ~$30–60, massive impact
📱

Control System

  • All Govee products run on one app — set scenes, schedules, sync
  • Alexa/Google compatible for voice control
  • Set up 5 scenes: Focus, Forest, Dragon, Arcane, Sleep
  • Gaming sync: Govee PC app detects screen colors automatically

Bathroom · 9'5" × 5'0"

Stone Forest Bath

Already has the right bones — standing shower, medicine cabinet mirror, blue-grey walls. The existing color is actually close. Here's exactly what to change and what to keep.

What's Already Working

The blue-grey wall color is actually close to your palette — it reads cool-forest. The standing shower with glass panel is sleek. The medicine cabinet mirror gives you storage. Don't tear this apart, just layer on top.

🔧

Hardware Swap — Instant Impact

  • Replace all chrome hardware with matte black — towel ring, towel bar, toilet paper holder, robe hook
  • Takes under an hour with a screwdriver, zero damage
  • Amazon: matte black bathroom hardware set ~$35–60
  • The chrome in the photos is the main thing aging the space — this fixes it immediately
💡

Lighting Upgrade

  • Replace the Hollywood-style bulb bar with a matte black bar fixture OR just swap bulbs to warm 2700K
  • Add a small RGB strip behind the mirror — plugs in, warm amber default, spa mode instantly
  • Battery LED candles on the counter — moisture-safe, real flicker
  • A Himalayan salt lamp on the back of the toilet tank = warm glow, on-theme, cheap
🪨

Texture + Surface

  • Peel-and-stick slate or stone tile on ONE wall — the wall behind the toilet is ideal (easy access, no steam damage)
  • Bamboo bath mat to replace the standard bath mat — ties to the Asian-forest theme
  • Dark hand towels + dark washcloths — charcoal or forest green replace the pale ones in the photos
  • A dark wooden tray on the counter to corral your skincare — looks luxe, keeps it organized
🌿

Plants + Nature

  • Eucalyptus bundle tied to shower head — lasts 2–3 weeks, smells incredible, looks stunning
  • Small snake plant or ZZ plant on back of toilet — handles humidity, low light
  • Bamboo stalks in a tall dark vase on the counter or floor corner
  • Keep it to 3 plants max — bathroom is small, overcrowding kills the calm
🧴

Skincare Station

  • Large dark bamboo or acacia tray on counter — your SkinMedica, ZO, and Glow Recipe displayed neatly
  • Under-sink: clear organizer bins for backup products + supplies
  • A small LED ring light mirror on the wall beside the medicine cabinet — for precise skincare application
  • Your product collection displayed neatly IS decor — it looks intentional and spa-like
🚿

Shower Details

  • Dark shower curtain if there's a rod, OR a dark shower liner inside the glass — makes the shower read darker and more luxe
  • Black corner shower caddy — replace any chrome ones
  • A small waterproof Bluetooth speaker in the shower (JBL Clip) — your music/DnD ambience follows you
  • Dark pumice stone or wooden soap dish on the shelf — small but on-theme
Loft · 27'0" × 11'10"

The Communal Tavern

Now that you've seen the actual space — the A-frame peak with the triangle window facing the forest — this is even better than planned. That window is the centerpiece of the whole loft.

The Triangle Window Is Everything

The gothic A-frame triangle window facing the tree line south is your loft's hero feature. Your DnD table goes directly in front of it. Whoever sits as Dungeon Master has a forest visible behind them through an arched window. This is free set dressing you cannot buy at any price.

🎲

Zone A — DnD Table (South End)

  • Round or rectangular dark table seats 5–6, positioned directly before the triangle window
  • Mixed seating: 2–3 chairs + Moroccan poufs for overflow — fits the tavern aesthetic perfectly
  • Overhead pendant light directly above — hung from the A-frame beam, warm and dimmable
  • Small rolling IKEA RÅSKOG cart beside the table: dice, snacks, rulebooks, candles
  • Dark felt table runner for mini play — rolls away in seconds when it's dinner mode
  • Fantasy map print or dragon art on the adjacent wall — the visual backdrop when facing the DM
📖

Zone B — Reading Nook (North End)

  • Deep armchair — velvet or textured, forest green or charcoal — near the railing where you look down into the living room
  • Tall bookshelf on the flat wall: dark wood, books + small decor + one dragon figure (your subtle loft presence)
  • Arc floor lamp over the chair — warm, dimmable, adjustable
  • Small side table: a candle, your drink, a dice tray
  • The railing running along the loft edge is already a design feature — a trailing plant along it reads like vines
🌉

The Threshold Between Zones

  • Two rugs define two zones — the gap between them IS the divider. No wall needed
  • A low console shelf or bench perpendicular to the wall between zones creates a soft visual break
  • The existing wood railing runs along one edge naturally — your loft already has built-in structure
  • String lights strung along the beam overhead unify both zones under one canopy
🤝

Communal Design Strategy

  • Dark forest tavern reads as universally sophisticated — roommates will love this, not feel invaded by it
  • Your dragon presence in the loft is subtle: one figure on the bookshelf, a fantasy art print. The bedroom is the full collection
  • The loft is shared investment territory — worth asking roommates to split the table and sofa cost
  • The existing A-frame character already does 70% of the work — you're just furnishing what's there

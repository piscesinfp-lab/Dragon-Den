Storage Strategy

The Three-Zone System

Two bedroom closets plus the bathroom under-sink. Each zone has one job. When everything has a designated home, the ADHD brain doesn't have to make decisions about where things go — it's already decided.

Large Closet

East wall, bedroom
  • All clothing — hung + folded
  • Shoes — floor level
  • Cosplay pieces as they grow
  • Accessories + bags

Small Closet (4×5)

Outside bathroom
  • Towels + linens
  • Skincare backup stock
  • Cleaning supplies
  • Toiletry overflow

Under-Sink

Bathroom
  • Daily skincare overflow
  • Cleaning products
  • Feminine hygiene
  • Backup toiletries

Murphy Bed Cabinets

South wall, bedroom
  • Bedside essentials
  • Current read + journal
  • Meds + small items
  • Charging cables
The ADHD Storage Rule

Closed storage hides chaos but also hides your stuff — you forget it exists. Open storage reminds you things are there but looks messy. The solution: closed storage for categories you access weekly or less, open/visible storage for daily-use items. Every drawer and bin gets a label. Not optional.

The Double Rod Hack

A single closet rod wastes the bottom half of the space. Adding a second rod below doubles your hanging capacity instantly.

  • Install a second rod using a closet rod doubler — hooks over the existing rod, no drilling needed (~$15)
  • Upper rod: Long items — dresses, coats, jumpsuits, anything that needs full length
  • Lower rod: Short items — tops, blouses, cardigans, anything that hits mid-thigh or above
  • Use matching dark velvet slim hangers throughout — non-slip, space-saving, looks intentional
  • Hang by category, then by color within each category — takes 20 minutes to set up, saves minutes every day
🗂️

Drawer Unit Inside Closet

A small 3–4 drawer unit on the floor right side replaces a dresser entirely — keeps folded clothes inside the closet, out of the room.

  • Drawer 1: Underwear + bras — use small dividers inside so nothing tangles
  • Drawer 2: Socks + tights — rolled, not folded flat
  • Drawer 3: Loungewear + sleepwear
  • Drawer 4 (if 4-drawer): Workout wear or seasonal overflow
  • IKEA ALEX or MALM 3-drawer — both fit inside a standard closet footprint
📦

Top Shelf Bins

  • 3 matching dark bins or baskets on the top shelf — each gets a label
  • Bin 1: Seasonal clothes — swap in/out twice a year
  • Bin 2: Cosplay overflow — pieces waiting for display or next use
  • Bin 3: Extras — extra bedding, spare towels, anything that doesn't live elsewhere
  • Dark woven bins from HomeGoods or Amazon — stay on the forest aesthetic even inside the closet
🎭

Cosplay Section — Starting Small

  • Since you're just starting: dedicate the right end of the upper rod to cosplay pieces that hang
  • A clear over-door organizer on the back of the closet door holds wigs, accessories, small props
  • As collection grows: one top shelf bin stays permanently cosplay — rotate what's active to the rod
  • Wigs: individual wig stands stored on top shelf or in labeled boxes — never crammed in a bag
  • When cosplay outgrows this: revisit with its own dedicated guide section
Closet Door — Replace With Dark Curtain

Per the master guide — swap the louvered closet doors for a ceiling-mounted curtain track (IKEA VIDGA) with a dark linen curtain. Easier to access than doors that swing into the room, looks architectural, and eliminates the visual clutter of closet door hardware. The curtain stays closed by default — out of sight, out of mind, which is exactly what ADHD needs.

Small Closet · 4'×5' · Outside Bathroom

The Utility Closet

4 feet wide, 5 feet deep — more space than it sounds. This becomes your household supply hub: towels, skincare backup, cleaning supplies, toiletry overflow. All of the above.

Towels + Linens (Shelf 1)

  • Roll towels rather than fold flat — takes up less space, easier to grab one without unraveling the stack
  • Two sets per person minimum: one in use, one clean and ready
  • Dark charcoal or forest green towels — they're already on the bathroom shopping list, store the spares here
  • Extra pillowcases and a spare sheet set folded flat at the back of the shelf
🧴

Skincare Backup (Shelf 2)

  • Clear labeled bins — one per category: Cleansers · Moisturizers · Treatments · SPF · Tools
  • When a product runs out at the bathroom counter, the backup is already here — no emergency runs
  • FIFO rule: new stock goes behind existing stock, use oldest first
  • Your SkinMedica and ZO lines are investments — storing them cool and dark (in this closet vs on a sunny counter) extends their shelf life
  • A small label maker (~$20) is worth every penny for this shelf
🧹

Cleaning Supplies (Floor)

  • A portable cleaning caddy on the floor — everything in one grab, carry room to room
  • Wall hooks on the inside wall: mop, broom, Swiffer — hang them, don't lean them (they fall)
  • Caddy contents: multi-surface spray, bathroom cleaner, glass cleaner, scrubber, microfiber cloths
  • A small tension rod inside the closet door holds spray bottles by their triggers — saves floor space
📋

Toiletry Overflow (Shelf 3)

  • Backup toilet paper, paper towels, feminine hygiene — all the stuff that needs a bulk home
  • One clear bin: "restock soon" — when something from the bathroom counter runs low, it goes in this bin as a visual reminder to grab from backup
  • Travel toiletry bag lives here too — pre-packed and ready, not scattered across the bathroom when you need it
Clothing System

Building From Scratch

You said you need a system — here's one that works specifically for ADHD. The goal is decision fatigue elimination: every item has a category, every category has a location, getting dressed takes under 2 minutes.

Hang everything you can
Folded items get forgotten. If it can hang without wrinkling — hang it. Tops, dresses, jackets, cardigans, jeans on clip hangers. Only underwear, socks, and loungewear go in drawers.
Category order on rod
Left to right: Jackets/coats → Dresses → Tops (long sleeve) → Tops (short sleeve) → Bottoms → Cosplay. Same order every time — muscle memory kicks in.
Color within category
Within each category: dark to light, left to right. Black → navy → grey → green → other. Takes one setup session, then you always know where your black top lives.
The return rule
Worn but not dirty items get a dedicated hook on the back of the closet door or a small valet hook on the wall — not on the floor, not on the chair. This is the ADHD chair problem solved: give the habit somewhere specific to land.
Laundry system
Two slim laundry bags: darks and lights, both hanging inside the closet. When a bag is full = laundry day. No laundry basket sitting on the floor collecting visual noise.
Seasonal rotation
Twice a year (spring + fall): pull out-of-season clothes off the rod, fold into the top shelf bin, pull the opposite season bin down and hang. 30 minutes, done for 6 months. The rod stays uncluttered.
The one-in one-out rule
Small space, limited rod space. Every time something new comes in, something old leaves. Keeps the system from collapsing over time — especially important as the cosplay collection grows.
Shoes · Under 10 Pairs

Shoe Storage

Under 10 pairs is very manageable. One good rack keeps them visible, accessible, and off the floor.

👟

The Setup

  • A 3-tier adjustable shoe rack on the left floor of the large closet — holds 9–12 pairs easily
  • Angled shelves keep shoes visible and easy to grab
  • Arrangement: everyday shoes at eye level (middle tier), occasion shoes top, slippers/house shoes bottom
  • Recommended: SONGMICS 3-tier adjustable — dark finish, sturdy, ~$25
  • Size 13W note: deep racks (14"+ depth) accommodate larger sizes better than shallow ones
📦

Overflow + Special Pairs

  • Any shoes that don't fit the daily rotation go in clear drop-front shoe boxes — stackable, visible without opening
  • Clear boxes on the top closet shelf keep special pairs dust-free and visible
  • IRIS USA clear shoe boxes are the standard — ~$3–5 each
  • Label each box on the front with a small label or photo — ADHD brain will thank you
🔧

Shoe Care Station

  • A small dark tray at the closet floor entrance: shoe horn, brush, and a neutral polish/cleaner
  • Keeps shoe care from becoming a drawer-hunting mission
  • A boot insert or boot form for any tall boots — they collapse and crease without support
  • Cedar shoe trees for leather or nicer shoes — absorb moisture and hold shape
Cosplay Storage

Starting Small, Scaling Up

Minimal right now — but building the right infrastructure now means your system doesn't collapse as the collection grows. Cosplay has specific storage needs: wigs, props, and garments all need different treatment.

👘

Garments

  • Cosplay pieces that hang go on the dedicated right section of the clothing rod — costume pieces on their own hangers, grouped by character
  • Delicate or structured pieces: garment bags to protect from dust and snags
  • Heavy or armored pieces that can't hang: folded with tissue paper inside structured shapes, stored in labeled bins on the top shelf
  • A small steamer (not iron) for wrinkled cosplay fabric — stored inside the closet near garments
💇

Wigs

  • Each wig on its own wig head — never stored in a bag or box without a form inside
  • Wig heads on the top closet shelf or a dedicated wall-mounted row of hooks
  • Alternatively: a tall multi-tier wig stand holds 6–8 wigs vertically, folds flat when not in use
  • Label each wig head with the character name — sounds obvious, saves confusion when you have 5+ wigs
  • Cover with a hairnet or light cloth when not in use — keeps dust off and style intact
⚔️

Props + Accessories

  • An over-door clear pocket organizer on the back of the closet door: small accessories, jewelry, ears, tails, clips per character
  • Label each pocket section by character or type
  • Larger props (swords, staffs, shields): a tall narrow bin or umbrella stand in the closet corner — leaned upright, not laid flat where they get crushed
  • Foam props and armor: stored flat in a wide shallow bin to prevent warping
📸

The Cosplay Inventory

  • Start a simple notes doc or photo album: one photo of each full cosplay laid flat, labeled with the character and what's complete vs missing
  • ADHD benefit: you can see your whole collection in 30 seconds without opening every bin
  • Tracks what accessories you still need to source for each look
  • Reference for packing when going to a con — no last-minute "where is the thing" panic
Bathroom · Under-Sink

Under-Sink Organization

The pipe under the sink eats into usable space — the solution is building around it with the right organizers rather than trying to shove bins in flat.

🪣

The Two-Tier Approach

  • A pull-out under-sink organizer with two tiers built around the pipe — specifically designed for this problem
  • Recommended: mDesign pull-out cabinet organizer or Lynk Professional slide-out shelf
  • Pull-out means you actually access the back of the cabinet instead of pushing things in and forgetting them
  • Front zone (easy reach): daily use backup items — what runs out most often
  • Back zone: bulk backup — less frequent access is fine here
🗄️

Zone It Out

  • Left side of pipe: Skincare — cleanser backup, moisturizer backup, treatment backups
  • Right side of pipe: Feminine hygiene, backup soap, backup shampoo/conditioner
  • Door interior: Tension rod across the door holds spray bottles — cleaning products hung by trigger
  • Small bin front-center: Trash bags, extra sponges, anything you grab while cleaning
🏷️

Label Everything

  • Clear bins with labels on the front — not the top, you look at things from the front
  • Categories: Skincare Backup · Hair · Body · Hygiene · Cleaning
  • A label maker is the single best investment for ADHD organization — Dymo LabelManager is ~$20
  • When a bin is empty = buy more. Simple restock trigger that doesn't require mental tracking
ADHD-Proof Design

Systems That Actually Work

Standard organization advice assumes a neurotypical brain. These rules are specifically designed around how ADHD actually operates — out of sight is out of mind, decision fatigue is real, and friction kills habits.

👁️

Visibility Over Concealment

  • If you can't see it, it doesn't exist to the ADHD brain — use clear bins wherever possible
  • Frequently used items stay visible on the counter, shelf, or hook — not buried in a drawer
  • The closet curtain stays open by default during the day — seeing your clothes helps you actually pick an outfit
  • One "catch-all" visible tray per room for things without a home yet — empties weekly, never grows permanently
⚡

Friction Reduction

  • The more steps it takes to put something away, the less likely it gets put away — eliminate steps
  • Laundry bags inside the closet vs a basket across the room = clothes actually make it off the floor
  • Hooks beat hangers for items you wear multiple times — robe, everyday jacket, bag
  • Pull-out organizers beat deep static shelves — you'll actually use what's in the back
  • Charging cables at exactly the spots where you use devices — not one central charging spot
🔄

Reset Rituals

  • A 10-minute Sunday reset: everything goes back to its home, the catch-all tray empties, laundry gets sorted
  • This works because it's timed and bounded — not "clean up" but "10 minutes, then done"
  • The room should be resettable in under 15 minutes if the systems are working — if it takes longer, something doesn't have a home yet
  • The dark aesthetic actually helps: clutter shows up immediately against the dark walls, which is a cue to reset sooner
🏷️

Label Everything. Seriously.

  • Every bin, every drawer, every shelf section — labeled on the front in clear text
  • This isn't just about finding things — it's about knowing where to PUT things back
  • A Dymo label maker takes 20 minutes to label an entire room and pays dividends forever
  • Photo labels on opaque bins (tape a small photo of the contents to the front) work better than text for visual thinkers
The ADHD Chair Problem — Solved

Every ADHD person has a chair, bed corner, or floor spot where "worn but not dirty" clothes pile up. The solution isn't willpower — it's giving that habit a designated landing spot. A small wall-mounted valet hook or a single freestanding hook inside the closet near the door becomes the official "limbo" spot. One hook, one item. When it's full, it's a visual cue to deal with it. The pile disappears because the habit has a home.
